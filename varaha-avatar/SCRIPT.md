# Varaha Avatar — The Boar Who Lifted the Earth

**Format:** 9:16 vertical (1080×1920, 30 fps) · **Length:** 90 s · **Narration:** soft female voice (Kokoro, blend of *af_heart* + *af_nicole*) · **Style:** "Luminous Myth" — flat 2D shapes in jewel-tone gradients (sapphire, crimson, violet, emerald, gold), gold-line mandalas, soft glows, slow camera pushes and cross-dissolves. Same look as the Matsya, Kurma and Rama films.

**Score & sound:** original procedural score — tanpura-style drone in D, a slow flute melody in raga Bhupali, harp plucks; a dark drone while the demon rules and the Earth is lost; a frame-drum pulse for the roar and the battle. Effects: conch, temple bell, chimes, waves, bubbles, thunder.

## Narration (full text)

Long ago, a demon named Hiranyaksha grew so powerful that nothing could harm him. He stormed through the three worlds, and even the gods fled before him. Drunk on his own strength, he seized the Earth herself, and dragged her down to the bottom of the cosmic ocean. The world went dark, and the gods cried out for help. Without the Earth, there was nowhere left for life to stand. Then, from the breath of Brahma the creator, a tiny boar appeared, no bigger than a thumb. In moments it grew as large as a mountain, and its roar shook the heavens. This was Varaha, Vishnu in the form of a mighty boar. He plunged into the dark waters, searching the deep for the lost Earth, and found her resting on the ocean floor, cold and silent. Gently, he lifted her onto his shining tusks. But Hiranyaksha blocked his path, swinging his mace. They fought a thunderous battle that shook all three worlds, until, with one final blow, Varaha struck the demon down. Then he rose from the sea, the Earth balanced on his tusks, and set her softly back in her place among the stars. The Earth goddess, Bhudevi, bowed in thanks. Forests grew, rivers ran, and life returned. This is Varaha, the third avatar of Vishnu, a reminder that no matter how deep the world sinks, it can always be lifted again.
