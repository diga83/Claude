## Narration (full text)

Long ago, a boy was born to the sage Jamadagni, with the calm of a priest, and the fire of a warrior. He prayed to Shiva for years, and Shiva gave him a blazing axe. From that day, he was called Parashurama, Rama with the axe. In those days, a king with a thousand arms ruled without mercy. One day he visited the sage's quiet home, and saw their wish granting cow. She could conjure a feast for a whole army, out of thin air. He wanted her, so he simply took her by force. When Parashurama returned, he stormed the palace alone, defeated the thousand armed king, and brought the cow home. But the king's sons took a cruel revenge, and his father was lost. Heartbroken, Parashurama made a vow, to free the earth from every tyrant. Twenty one times he crossed the land, and no cruel king could stand before his axe. His anger burned like a forest fire. But when it was over, he laid his anger down, and gave away everything he had won, and cast his axe into the sea, where the waters drew back, and new land rose. Then he climbed a mountain to meditate, where it is said he lives to this day, waiting to teach the heroes yet to come. This is Parashurama, the sixth avatar of Vishnu, a reminder that true strength protects the weak, and knows when to lay its weapon down.
