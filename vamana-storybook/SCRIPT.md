## Narration (full text)

Long ago, there lived a demon king named Bali, the grandson of gentle Prahlada. He was brave, and fair, and so generous that no one who asked ever left his door with empty hands. But his armies conquered the earth, and then the heavens, and the gods were driven from their home. Their mother, Aditi, could not bear it. She prayed to Vishnu, day after day. And Vishnu answered. He was born as her own son, a small, bright eyed boy named Vamana. One morning, King Bali held a great ceremony by the river, promising a gift to anyone who asked. Through the crowd came Vamana, carrying only a little umbrella and a water pot. Bali bowed to the child. Ask for anything, he said. Gold, elephants, a whole kingdom. Vamana smiled. I need only three steps of land, measured by my own feet. The court laughed. But Bali's teacher whispered, be careful, this is Vishnu himself. Bali answered, I have given my word. And he poured the water to seal his promise. The moment it touched his hands, Vamana began to grow, past the trees, past the clouds, past the stars. With his first step, he covered the whole earth. With his second, he covered the heavens. In all creation, there was nowhere left to stand. Then he looked down, and asked, where shall I place my third? Bali had nothing left, except himself. He knelt, and offered his own head. Vishnu's foot came down as gently as a blessing, and the last of Bali's pride melted away. He made Bali king of the world below, and promised to guard his door forever. And once every year, they say, Bali returns to visit the people who love him. That day is Onam. This is Vamana, the fifth avatar of Vishnu, a reminder that true greatness lies in humility, and in keeping your word.
