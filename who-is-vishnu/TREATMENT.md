# Who is Vishnu? (treatment)

**Format:** 1080×1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 170 s · English voice-over, warm female narrator (Kokoro `af_bella`, speed 0.88), burned-in subtitles + `.srt` · −14 LUFS
**Style:** Watercolor Brush. **Approach:** an explainer that answers its title. Symbols and animals, not portraits (the avatars are shown by their fish, tortoise, boar, split pillar and mane, parasol and footprints, axe, bow, flute, bodhi tree and plough, white horse). Facts follow the common Puranic list of the Dashavatara; the ninth varies between lists (the Buddha or Balarama) and the film says so; *avatara* means "descent".

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **The ten as a ring of emblems** — already in *The Three Answers*; rejected.
2. **One avatar told in depth** — already *An Ocean, a Step, a Stone* (Vamana) and *The Patient Tortoise* (Kurma); rejected.
3. **A climb through one painted column (chosen).** The question is written on blank paper and becomes a blue wash, the colour of sky and sea. The camera dives to the bottom of a tall column and climbs it band by band: the flood fish in the deep, the tortoise at the surface, the boar on the shore, the man-lion at a doorway at dusk, the dwarf, the forest warrior, the prince by a walled city, the cowherd in a meadow, the teacher under a bodhi tree, and the rider in a gold sky. The palette carries the climb: blues, then dusk, then greens, then gold. At the end the ten gather as panels on one page, and a gold line *descends* through them, which is the meaning of "avatar", then loops back to the top: again, and again.

## 2. Logline and arc
*Ask who Vishnu is and you get ten answers; together they say one thing: he keeps coming down.*
Setup: the question, the preserver, the colour. Development: the ten, climbing from water to sky. Turn: the pattern (water → land, animal → human → sage). Ending: the word itself, a descent, drawn as a line through all ten.

## 4. Sound
72 BPM; harp, bowed vibraphone, cello, flute, glockenspiel. **The harmony climbs one step of D major per band**, so the music rises with the camera. A glockenspiel note greets each band (placed before the voice, never on it). Foley per band: rain and waves, deep water, a roar of colour for the man-lion, footsteps, leaves, a river and a cow, birds, wind and hooves. At the end the glockenspiel walks *down* the scale as the gold line descends, and a tubular bell sounds under the title.
