# Vamana Avatar — Three Steps That Measured the Universe

**Format:** 9:16 vertical (1080×1920, 30 fps) · **Length:** 90 s · **Narration:** soft female voice (Kokoro, blend of *af_heart* + *af_nicole*) · **Style:** "Luminous Myth" — flat 2D shapes in jewel-tone gradients (saffron, royal purple, magenta, sapphire, gold), gold-line mandalas, soft glows, slow camera pushes and cross-dissolves. Same look as the Matsya, Kurma, Varaha and Rama films.

**Score & sound:** original procedural score — tanpura-style drone in D, a slow flute melody in raga Bhupali, harp plucks; a darker drone when the gods lose heaven and for the teacher's warning; a frame-drum pulse through the growth and the two great strides. Effects: conch, temple bell, chimes, fire, water, thunder.

## Narration (full text)

Long ago, a demon king named Bali ruled so well that his power spread across all three worlds. Even the gods lost their heaven, and their mother Aditi prayed to Vishnu for help. So Vishnu was born as her son, a small and smiling boy named Vamana. One day, King Bali held a grand ceremony, promising a gift to anyone who asked. Vamana walked in, carrying only a little umbrella and a water pot. Bali smiled. Ask for anything, gold, elephants, a whole kingdom. But the boy asked only for three steps of land, measured by his own tiny feet. Bali's teacher warned him, this is no ordinary child. But Bali had given his word, and he poured the water to seal the gift. The moment it touched his hands, Vamana began to grow, past the trees, past the clouds, past the stars. With his first step, he covered the whole earth. With his second, he covered the heavens and all the stars. Then he asked, where shall I place my third? The universe was his, and nothing was left to give. Bali bowed low, and offered his own head. Moved by his humility, Vishnu blessed him, made him king of the world below, and promised to guard his door forever. This is Vamana, the fifth avatar of Vishnu, a reminder that true greatness is found in humility, and in keeping your word.
