# Rama Avatar — The Prince Who Chose What Was Right

**Format:** 9:16 vertical (1080×1920, 30 fps) · **Length:** 120 s · **Narration:** soft female voice (Kokoro, blend of *af_heart* + *af_nicole*) · **Style:** "Luminous Myth" — flat 2D shapes in jewel-tone gradients (saffron, magenta, royal purple, emerald, gold), gold-line mandalas, soft glows, slow camera pushes and cross-dissolves. ## Narration (full text)

Long ago, in the golden city of Ayodhya, a prince was born to King Dasharatha, and his name was Rama. He was Vishnu himself, the seventh avatar, born to end the terror of Ravana, the ten headed king of Lanka. Rama grew up brave and gentle, and when he lifted the mighty bow of Shiva, it snapped in two, and he won the hand of Princess Sita. But on the eve of his crowning, his stepmother claimed an old promise from the king, fourteen years of exile in the forest. Rama simply smiled and obeyed, and Sita and his brother Lakshmana walked beside him. One day, a dazzling golden deer lured Rama away, and Ravana swooped down in his flying chariot, and carried Sita off across the sea. The brave old eagle Jatayu fought to stop him, but could not. Heartbroken, Rama searched the forests, until he met Hanuman, the mighty monkey hero, who vowed to find her. With one giant leap, Hanuman crossed the ocean, found Sita in a garden in Lanka, and gave her Rama's ring. Then he set the demon city ablaze with his burning tail. Back on the shore, an army of monkeys built a bridge of floating stones, each one carved with Rama's name. They marched into Lanka, and a great battle shook the earth. At last, Rama drew a blazing arrow, and Ravana fell. Rama and Sita were together again, and after fourteen long years, they flew home to Ayodhya, where every street glowed with tiny lamps. That night of lights is still celebrated as Diwali. Rama was crowned king, and his reign became an age of peace. This is Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu, a reminder to do what is right, even when it is hard, because the light always finds its way home.

- Later tradition (e.g. Ramcharitmanas and popular retellings): stones inscribed with Rama's name floating; the lamps of Ayodhya as the origin of Diwali
