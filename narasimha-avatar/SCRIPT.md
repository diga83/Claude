# Narasimha Avatar — The Lion Who Stepped Out of a Pillar

**Format:** 9:16 vertical (1080×1920, 30 fps) · **Length:** 91 s · **Narration:** soft female voice (Kokoro, blend of *af_heart* + *af_nicole*) · **Style:** "Luminous Myth" — flat 2D shapes in jewel-tone gradients (crimson, flame gold, twilight violet), gold-line mandalas, soft glows, slow camera pushes and cross-dissolves. Same look as the other films in the series.

**Visual device:** the five conditions of the wish appear as five round badges (man/beast, day/night, indoors/outdoors, earth/sky, weapon). They ring the demon king like a shield when he asks for them, then sit along the top of the frame and break one by one as Narasimha meets each condition.

**Score & sound:** original procedural score — tanpura-style drone in D, a slow flute melody in raga Bhupali, harp plucks; a dark drone for the wish, the trials and the king's rage; a frame-drum pulse from the cracking pillar to the end of the tyrant. ## Narration (full text)

Long ago, a demon king named Hiranyakashipu prayed for years, until Brahma offered him a wish. He asked that no man or beast could harm him, by day or by night, indoors or outdoors, on the earth or in the sky, by any weapon. Feeling invincible, he ordered the whole world to worship him alone. But his own little son, Prahlada, loved Vishnu with all his heart. The furious king tried everything to break him, wild elephants, serpents, even fire. Each time, the boy simply prayed, and walked away unharmed. At last the king roared, where is your Vishnu? Is he in this pillar? He is everywhere, said Prahlada. The king struck the pillar, and it cracked with a sound like thunder. Out stepped Narasimha, half man, half lion, neither man nor beast. It was twilight, neither day nor night. He stood on the doorstep, neither indoors nor outdoors, lifted the king onto his lap, neither earth nor sky, and with his claws alone, he ended the tyrant's reign. Every word of the wish was kept, and still it could not save him. Only little Prahlada was unafraid. He walked up, and the lion's fury melted into a smile. Narasimha blessed the boy, and made him king. This is Narasimha, the fourth avatar of Vishnu, a reminder that no power can shield cruelty, and that faith fears nothing.
