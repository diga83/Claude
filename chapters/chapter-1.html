1. What Is a Digital Device?

A digital device is any electronic device that processes, stores, or transmits data in binary form — using 0s and 1s. Every device covered in this chapter works with digital data in some way.

💡 Digital vs Analogue — Know the Difference

Digital: Data stored and processed as discrete binary values (0 or 1). All modern computing devices are digital.

Analogue: Data represented as a continuous signal (not binary). Examples: vinyl records, traditional clock hands, mercury thermometers.

Exam note: The spec does not require deep knowledge of analogue vs digital, but knowing this distinction helps you understand WHY all modern devices are called 'digital'.

2.2 Mainframe Computers & Embedded Microprocessors [Spec 1.1.1]

📋 SPEC 1.1.1

Be aware that mainframe computers are used for complex processing tasks and microprocessors are embedded in products such as washing machines.

Mainframe Computers

What it is: A very large, powerful computer system designed to handle enormous amounts of data and many thousands of simultaneous users. Key features:

  • Processes millions of transactions per second
  • Supports thousands of simultaneous remote users
  • Extremely high reliability — runs 24/7/365
  • Kept in specialist climate-controlled data centres Real-life examples:
  • Banks — process millions of card transactions daily
  • Airlines — manage worldwide booking systems
  • Government — census data processing, tax records

Embedded Microprocessors

What it is: A small processing chip built directly into a product or appliance to automatically control its specific functions. Key features:

  • Performs one specific, limited task
  • Very small, low power consumption
  • Usually not programmable by the end user
  • Runs automatically when the device is switched on Real-life examples:
  • Washing machine — controls wash cycle, temperature
  • Microwave oven — controls power level and timer
  • Car engine management unit — controls fuel injection
⚡ EXAM TIP

Mainframe vs embedded — the exam often asks for ONE example of each. Remember: mainframes serve MANY users doing COMPLEX processing tasks. Embedded microprocessors do ONE simple automated task inside a household product. Never say a mainframe is just 'a very fast computer' — the key is simultaneous users and massive data volume.

2.3 Desktop & Laptop Computers [Spec 1.1.2]

📋 SPEC 1.1.2

Understand that laptop and desktop computers are types of personal computers. Some laptops are used as desktop replacements.

Desktop Computer

Fixed location — sits on a desk permanently

  • Very high performance for the price
  • Easily upgraded (RAM, GPU, extra storage)
  • Requires mains power — no battery
  • Larger screen; separate keyboard and mouse
  • Not portable — cannot be moved easily Best for: Office workers, gamers, video editors who stay in one location

Laptop Computer

Portable — use anywhere, battery-powered

  • Built-in screen, keyboard, and trackpad
  • Battery life: 4–12+ hours typical
  • Limited upgrade options — often sealed units
  • Can act as desktop replacement with external monitor + keyboard
  • Higher cost per unit of performance vs desktop Best for: Students, business travellers, remote workers needing flexibility
📌 What Is a Desktop Replacement Laptop?

Some high-performance laptops (e.g. Apple MacBook Pro 16", Dell XPS 15) are powerful enough to completely replace a desktop computer for professional use.

Setup: The laptop is connected to a large external monitor, a full-size keyboard, and a mouse. It stays on the desk most of the time and is only unplugged for occasional travel.

Why choose this? One device for both desk and travel use. Avoids buying and maintaining two separate machines. Saves cost and simplifies data management.

Exam tip: If asked 'why might someone use a laptop as a desktop replacement?' — the answer must mention: versatility (one device), portability when needed, connects to external peripherals.

2.4 Smartphones & Mobile Phones [Spec 1.1.3]

📋 SPEC 1.1.3

Know about types of mobile phones: smartphones and specialist phones and how they connect to the network (SIM).

📡 The SIM Card — How Smartphones Connect to Networks

SIM = Subscriber Identity Module: A small chip that stores the user's identity on the mobile network operator's system (e.g. Vodafone, AT&T, Ooredoo).

1. The SIM stores the phone number and a unique authentication key for the network

2. When a call or data session starts, the SIM authenticates the device with the network

3. Mobile data (4G/5G) uses the SIM to route internet traffic through the mobile network

4. Wi-Fi connects through a wireless access point — does NOT use the SIM at all

eSIM: A virtual, built-in SIM — no physical card needed. Network credentials downloaded digitally. Used in modern iPhones and many Android phones.

A smartphone is the ultimate multifunctional device — it combines functions that previously required many separate devices:

SMARTPHONE = ONE DEVICE, MANY FUNCTIONS
Phone / SIM Network Make and receive calls and texts via the mobile networkCamera & Video Take high-resolution photos and record video in HD/4K
GPS Navigation Real-time location tracking and route guidance via satelliteMusic & Media Player Stream or play stored audio and video
Web Browser Access any website over Wi-Fi or 4G mobile dataEmail & Messaging Send text, image, audio, video messages over internet
Games Console Download and play games — replacing handheld consolesCalculator, Clock, Notes Utility apps replacing separate physical devices
⚡ EXAM TIP

SIM card exam questions are common. Key facts: (1) SIM identifies the device on the MOBILE NETWORK. (2) Wi-Fi does NOT use the SIM. (3) A smartphone can connect to the internet via EITHER 4G/5G (SIM) OR Wi-Fi. If asked for two methods of internet connectivity for a smartphone, always give both.

2.7 Navigation Aids (GPS) [Spec 1.1.6]

📋 SPEC 1.1.6

Know about navigation aids and how they are used.

GPS (Global Positioning System) is a network of 24+ satellites that orbit Earth and allow any GPS-enabled device to calculate its exact geographical location anywhere in the world.

How GPS Works — Step by Step

StepWhat HappensDetail
1. Satellites transmit24+ GPS satellites orbit Earth sending radio signalsEach signal contains satellite position + exact time of transmission
2. Device receivesGPS receiver in device picks up signals from 3+ satellitesMore satellites = more accurate; 4+ gives 3D position including altitude
3. TriangulationDevice calculates distance to each satellite using signal travel timeSpeed of light x time delay = distance. 3 distances = one unique position
4. Map overlayCalculated position displayed on digital map softwareGoogle Maps, Apple Maps, TomTom software renders the location visually
5. Live updatesInternet-connected GPS downloads live traffic and road changesUses mobile data (4G/5G) or Wi-Fi — NOT the GPS signal itself
⚠️ GPS vs Internet Maps — A Critical Distinction

GPS: RECEIVES signals from satellites. Works without internet. Does NOT transmit data back — it is receive-only.

Internet-connected navigation (e.g. Google Maps): Uses GPS for location AND downloads maps, live traffic, and directions via mobile data (4G/5G). Needs both GPS AND internet.

Standalone GPS device (e.g. TomTom): Uses GPS for location and pre-loaded maps. Does NOT need internet — maps are stored locally on the device.

Exam trap: Never say 'GPS sends data to satellites' — GPS is RECEIVE-ONLY. Satellites transmit DOWN; your device only receives.

5. Choosing the Right Device — Scenario Practice [Spec 1.2.2]

In the exam you will be given a scenario and asked to identify a suitable device and explain why. Always use this framework: NAME THE DEVICE → NAME A FEATURE → EXPLAIN WHY THAT FEATURE MATTERS IN THIS SPECIFIC SCENARIO.

🎯 Answer Framework for Scenario Questions

Step 1. Name the device: Be specific — 'tablet device' not just 'tablet'.

Step 2. Name the feature: Use one of the 9 official feature names.

Step 3. Explain how: Say HOW that feature helps in the specific situation described.

Step 4. Repeat: Give a second reason using a different feature (required for 4-mark answers).

Example answer: 'A tablet would be suitable [Step 1] because it has high portability [Step 2], meaning the nurse can carry it between wards without difficulty [Step 3]. Additionally, its touchscreen user interface [Step 2] allows quick record navigation without a separate keyboard [Step 3].'

7. Model Answers

Study the structure of each answer. Notice how features are named first, then explained with 'because' or 'meaning' — this is what earns the second mark on each point.

✎ MODEL ANSWER

[3 marks]

Question: Explain what is meant by the term 'convergence' in digital devices. Give one example. [3 marks]

Convergence refers to the process by which different types of digital devices become increasingly similar in their features and functionality over time (1). For example, smartphones and tablet devices now offer almost identical features — both include touchscreens, cameras, GPS, internet access, and the ability to run apps (1). This means the distinction between the two device types has become much less clear, as each can now perform tasks that previously required the other (1).

Why this scores full marks: ✓ Clear definition — not just 'they become more similar', but what the process involves and over what context ✓ Named example (smartphones and tablets) with actual features listed — not just 'they are similar now' ✓ Consequence explained — the boundary between devices has blurred — earns the third mark

✎ MODEL ANSWER

[4 marks]

Question: A nurse works in a hospital and needs to access patient records while moving between wards. Identify a suitable device and explain TWO reasons why it is suitable. [4 marks]

A tablet device would be suitable for the nurse. Firstly, a tablet has high portability (1) — it is lightweight and thin, allowing the nurse to carry it easily between wards during a busy shift without it being a hindrance (1). Secondly, a tablet has a touchscreen user interface (1) — this allows the nurse to quickly navigate patient records by tapping the screen, even while wearing medical gloves, without requiring a separate keyboard or mouse (1).

Why this scores full marks: ✓ Device clearly named ('tablet device' — specific, not just 'tablet') ✓ Feature 1 named (portability) and developed with 'allowing' — linked to nurse's specific context ✓ Feature 2 named (touchscreen UI) and developed with scenario detail (gloves, moving between wards) ✓ Both explanations are scenario-specific, not generic — this is what earns the second mark each time

✎ MODEL ANSWER

[4 marks]

Question: Explain what is meant by a 'multifunctional device'. Give one advantage and one disadvantage. [4 marks]

A multifunctional device is a single digital device that combines the features and functions of several different devices into one (1). One advantage is that the user does not need to buy multiple separate devices (1), saving money and meaning only one device needs to be carried at any time (1). One disadvantage is that if the multifunctional device is lost or breaks down, the user loses access to all of its functions simultaneously (1), whereas with separate devices only one function would be affected.

Why this scores full marks: ✓ Definition is complete and precise — 'combines features of several devices into one' ✓ Advantage explained with consequence ('saving money', 'one device to carry') — not just named ✓ Disadvantage explained with consequence — the knock-on effect of losing ALL functions at once ✓ 'Whereas' used to contrast — this type of linking word naturally develops the point and earns marks

8. Common Mistakes

✗ COMMON MISTAKES — DON'T DO THESE!

✗ Saying GPS 'sends data to satellites' — GPS is RECEIVE-ONLY. Satellites transmit signals DOWN to your device. Your device only receives; it never transmits back to them.

✗ Confusing SIM card with Wi-Fi — SIM connects to the MOBILE NETWORK (4G/5G). Wi-Fi connects through a wireless access point. They are two completely different systems.

✗ Writing 'it is portable' without explaining what portability ENABLES — always say 'which means the user can...' to develop the point and earn the second mark.

✗ Forgetting that some laptops are 'desktop replacements' — this is specifically in spec 1.1.2 and may appear in scenario questions about choosing between a desktop and a laptop.

✗ Describing convergence as 'devices getting better over time' — convergence is about different device TYPES becoming MORE SIMILAR to each other, not just improving.

✗ Confusing multifunctional with convergence — multifunctional = one device does many jobs. Convergence = different device types become similar over time. Know both definitions clearly.

✗ Writing 'it has connectivity' as a feature — always be specific: 'it has Wi-Fi connectivity, which allows the user to...' Vague feature names score 0.

✗ Saying a mainframe is 'just a very fast computer' — the exam expects you to focus on: handles MANY SIMULTANEOUS USERS, MASSIVE DATA VOLUMES, and EXTREMELY HIGH RELIABILITY.

10. Quick Reference — Key Terms & Revision

★ QUICK REFERENCE — KEY TERMS

Digital Device: Any electronic device that processes, stores, or transmits data in binary (0s and 1s)

Mainframe Computer: A very large, powerful computer used by big organisations for high-volume complex processing with thousands of simultaneous users

Embedded Microprocessor: A small chip built into an everyday product (e.g. washing machine) to control its automated functions

Personal Computer (PC): A computer designed for individual use — includes both desktops and laptops

Laptop (Desktop Replacement): A high-performance laptop used permanently at a desk with external monitor and keyboard, acting as a substitute for a desktop

Smartphone: A mobile phone with advanced features including touchscreen, apps, camera, GPS, and mobile internet via SIM card

SIM Card: Subscriber Identity Module — a chip that identifies the device on the mobile network and enables calls and mobile data

Tablet: A portable touchscreen device; larger than a smartphone but lighter and thinner than a laptop

GPS: Global Positioning System — receives signals from satellites to calculate exact geographical location. Receive-only — does not transmit to satellites

Multifunctional Device: A device that combines the functions of several separate devices into one (e.g. smartphone)

Convergence: The process by which different types of digital devices become increasingly similar in their features over time

Portability: How easily a device can be carried and used in different locations

Performance: Processing speed and power of a device — measured in GHz (CPU) and GB (RAM)

Connectivity: The range of methods by which a device connects to networks and other devices

Expansion Capability: Whether additional hardware (RAM, storage, GPU, peripherals) can be added to the device

Security Features: Built-in hardware or software that protects the device and its data from unauthorised access

⚡ 9 DEVICE FEATURES — Quick Memorisation List

S Storage — how much data can be stored; whether it can be expanded
