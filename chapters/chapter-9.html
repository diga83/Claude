📌 Online Shopping vs Traditional Retail — The Shift

1990s: Most shopping done in physical stores. Online shopping experimental and not trusted.

2000s: Amazon establishes trust; eBay creates peer-to-peer marketplace; secure HTTPS checkout matures.

2010s: Smartphones enable anywhere/anytime shopping; same-day delivery; apps replace websites for many.

2020s: COVID-19 accelerates digital shift; grocery delivery normalised; AR try-before-you-buy; subscription models dominate.

Today: UK e-commerce accounts for ~27% of all retail sales. Pure online retailers like ASOS compete directly with traditional fashion chains.

💡 How Online Business Models Differ from Traditional

Bricks-and-mortar only: Physical shop; fixed hours; local customers; high rent and staffing costs; limited reach.

Clicks-and-mortar: Both physical store and online shop — customers can buy either way; collect in store; return in store.

Pure online (e-tailer): No physical stores; lower costs; global reach; dependent entirely on logistics and digital systems.

Platform model: Organisation provides the marketplace (eBay, Amazon Marketplace, Etsy) and takes commission; others do the selling.

Subscription model: Recurring revenue (Netflix, Spotify, Amazon Prime); predictable income; high customer lifetime value.

Freemium model: Basic service free; premium features paid (Spotify Free vs Premium; LinkedIn Basic vs Premium).

⚠️ Cookies and the Law — GDPR Cookie Consent

What the law requires: Websites operating in the UK/EU must ask for consent before placing non-essential cookies on a user's device.

Essential cookies: Permitted without consent — needed for the site to function (e.g. keeping you logged in, session basket).

Non-essential cookies: Require explicit opt-in — includes analytics, advertising and tracking cookies.

What the pop-up is: The 'We use cookies' banner you see on websites — you must be able to reject all non-essential cookies.

Third-party cookies: Being phased out by Chrome (Google) and blocked by Firefox/Safari — advertising industry response: fingerprinting.

Exam note: If asked why websites show cookie consent banners — answer: legal requirement under GDPR/data protection law; users must consent to non-essential cookies before they are placed.

5. Targeted Marketing & Personalisation [Spec 4.2.4]

📋 SPEC 4.2.4

Know about targeted marketing and personalisation techniques.

Targeted marketing uses data collected about users to deliver personalised advertising and promotions. This is one of the primary commercial reasons for the extensive data collection described in section 4.

Personalised Recommendations AI analyses purchase history and browsing to suggest products likely to interest this specific user e.g. 'Customers who bought this also bought...' (Amazon); Spotify 'Discover Weekly' playlist + Relevant suggestions save browsing time; higher chance of finding useful products - Creates filter bubble; encourages over-spending; privacy intrusiveRetargeting Ads Ads for products you viewed appear on other websites and social media you subsequently visit e.g. Viewed shoes on ASOS; same shoes appear in ads on Facebook and news sites + Reminds customer of products they were considering; brings back window shoppers - Feels intrusive and can be unsettling; follows users across unrelated websitesEmail Personalisation Emails address customer by name; contain products relevant to their history; send at optimal times e.g. 'Hi Mia, your favourite brand just added new items' with products in their size/style + More relevant than generic newsletters; feels personal and valued - May feel too personal; relies on storing detailed personal data
Dynamic Pricing Prices change automatically based on demand, competitor prices, time of day, or customer data e.g. Flight prices higher when searched from expensive laptop (assumed wealth); hotel prices rise at peak times + Businesses maximise revenue; customers can find deals at low-demand times - Same customer pays different prices; can feel unfair or discriminatoryLoyalty / Rewards Programmes Track every purchase; reward customers with points, discounts, exclusive offers e.g. Tesco Clubcard; Costa Coffee loyalty app; Amazon Prime membership + Rewards frequent customers; encourages brand loyalty; provides rich data - Data collected extensively; manipulates purchase behaviour; points expireSocial Proof & Reviews Display ratings, review counts and 'best seller' labels to influence purchase decisions e.g. '4.7 stars from 12,000 reviews'; '#1 Bestseller in Electronics' + Helps decision-making; highlights quality products; reduces purchase anxiety - Reviews can be fake; bestseller labels manipulated; social pressure to conform
📌 The Data-to-Marketing Pipeline

Step 1. Data collected: Browsing, purchases, searches, location, social media, device type

Step 2. Data analysed: AI builds a profile: demographics, interests, purchase intent, price sensitivity

Step 3. Segmentation: User placed in audience segments (e.g. 'fitness enthusiast', 'bargain hunter', 'tech professional')

Step 4. Ad targeting: Specific ads served to specific segments on social media, search engines, websites

Step 5. Measurement: Click-through rates, conversions measured; AI optimises future targeting

Why this matters for privacy: This pipeline runs invisibly — users rarely realise how much data is collected and how it shapes what they see online.

⚡ EXAM TIP

'Describe how a retailer could use transactional data to target marketing.' Answer: The retailer collects data about which products a customer viewed and purchased (1). This data is analysed to build a customer profile — for example, identifying that they regularly buy sports equipment (1). The retailer then sends personalised emails (1) featuring new products in the sports category, or shows targeted ads for these products when the customer visits other websites (1).

📌 When Local Services Are Better — Don't Ignore This

Emergency services: Cannot call a website for an ambulance — local physical emergency services are irreplaceable.

Complex personal advice: A doctor, solicitor or financial adviser provides tailored professional advice that no website can fully replicate.

Immediate need: If you need a product today, a local shop serves you immediately — next-day delivery still means waiting.

No internet access: Rural areas, elderly people, or technical failures mean local services remain essential for accessibility.

Community and social value: Local shops and services employ local people, create community spaces, and contribute to local economic wellbeing.

Exam tip: Questions about 'whether online services should replace local services' require BOTH sides. Arguments for local services score as many marks as arguments for online.

7. Cloud Computing & Hosted Applications [Spec 4.3.1]

📋 SPEC 4.3.1

Understand the impact of cloud-based services: hosted applications, storage.

Cloud computing means delivering computing services — servers, storage, databases, software, analytics — over the internet ('the cloud') from remote data centres, rather than on local hardware.

USER Any device Laptop │ Tablet │ Phone │ Chromebook │ Smart TV↔ InternetCLOUD Remote servers Applications │ Storage │ Processing │ Data↔ ResultOUTPUT Accessible anywhere Files │ Apps │ Email │ Video │ Docs

7.1 Types of Cloud Services

SaaS Software as a Service Complete software applications delivered over the internet — no installation needed e.g. Google Workspace (Docs/Sheets/Gmail); Microsoft 365 online; Salesforce CRM; Zoom; Slack; Spotify Used by: End users who want to use applications without managing infrastructurePaaS Platform as a Service Development platform including tools, databases and infrastructure — developers build apps without managing hardware e.g. Google App Engine; Microsoft Azure; Heroku; AWS Elastic Beanstalk Used by: Developers building web applications; startups needing scalable backend
IaaS Infrastructure as a Service Virtual hardware (servers, networking, storage) rented on demand — organisation manages everything else e.g. Amazon Web Services (EC2); Microsoft Azure VMs; Google Compute Engine Used by: Organisations needing flexible compute power; enterprises replacing physical serversStorage Cloud Storage File storage on remote servers — accessible from any device e.g. Google Drive; iCloud; OneDrive; Dropbox; Amazon S3 Used by: Individual file access; backup; collaboration; media streaming
💡 Why the Cloud Changed Everything

Before cloud: A company wanting to launch a web service needed to buy servers, install them, hire IT staff to maintain them, and wait months before launching.

With cloud: The same company can launch in hours using rented AWS/Azure servers; scale up or down instantly; pay only for what they use; access globally.

For individuals: All your photos, music, documents available on every device without moving files manually. Lose your phone — data still safe in cloud.

For education: Chromebooks work on cloud apps — cheap devices work because processing and storage are online. Schools save thousands on hardware.

Key exam point: Cloud does not mean 'wireless' or 'virtual' — it specifically means services delivered from REMOTE SERVERS via the internet. Always define it precisely.

📌 Cloud Storage Scenario Decision Framework

Choose cloud storage when: Multiple devices need access to same files; real-time collaboration; automatic backup needed; disaster recovery required; team is geographically distributed.

Choose local storage when: Sensitive data that must not leave premises; internet connection is unreliable; large files (video editing) need fast local access; no ongoing subscription cost desired.

Hybrid approach: Many organisations use BOTH — critical documents in cloud for collaboration and backup; large working files stored locally for speed; archives backed up to cold cloud storage.

Security responsibility: Cloud: provider manages physical security of data centres but users responsible for account security. Local: user/organisation responsible for ALL security — backup, physical security, encryption.

10. Model Answers

Study the structure carefully. Every impact answer needs both sides. Every compare answer needs named features with explained differences.

✎ MODEL ANSWER

[4 marks]

Q: Explain how cookies are used by an online retailer to improve the shopping experience. [4 marks]

When a customer first visits the retailer's website, the server places a small text file called a cookie on the customer's device (1). This cookie records information about the customer's behaviour — such as which products they viewed, items added to their basket, and how long they spent on each page (1). When the customer returns to the site, the server reads the cookie and uses this data to personalise their experience — for example, displaying recommended products based on their previous browsing history (1). The cookie also restores their shopping session, keeping previously added basket items so they do not have to start their search again (1).

Why this scores full marks: ✓ Four distinct marks: cookie placed (1), data recorded (1), personalisation used (1), session restored (1) ✓ Answer describes the mechanism — not just 'cookies track you' ✓ Specific examples given (products viewed, basket items, recommendations) ✓ All four marks linked to the shopping experience context from the question

✎ MODEL ANSWER

[4 marks]

Q: Describe TWO advantages and TWO disadvantages of using cloud storage instead of local storage for a student's school files. [4 marks]

Advantage 1: Cloud storage can be accessed from any device with internet access (1), which means the student can work on their files at home, in school, or at a library without needing to carry a USB drive or remember to copy files (1). Advantage 2: Cloud storage is automatically backed up by the provider (1), so if the student's laptop is lost or damaged, their files are not lost and can be restored immediately from the cloud (1). Disadvantage 1: Cloud storage requires an internet connection to access files (1), which means if the student has no Wi-Fi or mobile data, they cannot access their work — for example, on a flight or in an area with poor signal (1). Disadvantage 2: The student's files are stored on a third-party company's servers (1), which means they have less control over privacy and must trust the provider to keep their data secure and not share it with others (1).

Why this scores full marks: ✓ Exactly two advantages and two disadvantages — follows the question structure ✓ Each point earns 2 marks: the advantage/disadvantage named (1) + explanation with example (1) ✓ All points linked to the student/school context — not generic cloud statements ✓ Disadvantage 2 addresses privacy — important and often missed

✎ MODEL ANSWER

[4 marks]

Q: Explain the difference between a hosted application and locally installed software. Give one advantage of each for a small business. [4 marks]

A hosted application (e.g. Google Docs) runs in a web browser and the software and data are stored on remote cloud servers — no installation is needed on the user's device (1). A locally installed application (e.g. Microsoft Word) is downloaded and installed on the user's own device and runs using that device's processor, RAM and storage (1). Advantage of hosted: The hosted application is automatically updated by the developer centrally (1), so all staff in the small business always have the latest version without the IT burden of updating individual computers (1). Advantage of locally installed: Locally installed software works offline without an internet connection (1), which means staff can continue working even during an internet outage — important for a small business that cannot afford downtime (1).

Why this scores full marks: ✓ Clear distinction drawn between both types — cloud server vs device; browser vs installation ✓ Each advantage earns 2 marks: advantage named (1) + consequence linked to small business context (1) ✓ Business-relevant examples used ('all staff', 'IT burden', 'cannot afford downtime') ✓ Four marks from two advantages each earning 2 — structured perfectly

11. Common Mistakes

✗ COMMON MISTAKES

✗ Describing all online shopping features without naming the spec-specific terms — the spec says basket, checkout, secure payment and product catalogue. Use these exact terms when answering shopping-related questions.

✗ Saying 'cloud means wireless' — cloud computing means services delivered from REMOTE SERVERS via the internet. A wired ethernet computer can use cloud services. 'Wireless' and 'cloud' are completely different concepts.

✗ Saying cookies are viruses or malware — cookies are small text files; they cannot execute code or damage your device. They are a data collection mechanism, not a security threat in themselves.

✗ Only giving advantages of online services and forgetting drawbacks — the spec requires understanding of BOTH. Local services have genuine advantages (personal advice, immediate access, community value) that must be discussed.

✗ Confusing hosted applications with cloud storage — cloud storage (Google Drive) stores files. Hosted applications (Google Docs) are software that runs in the browser. They are different things that can work together.

✗ Saying locally installed software 'cannot be used online' — locally installed software CAN save to the cloud and CAN use internet features. The key distinction is WHERE the software RUNS (local device vs cloud server).

✗ Giving only one side of impact questions — impact questions (4.2.1, 4.2.2) always require both positive AND negative. A 4-mark answer expecting 'two impacts' means one positive + one negative (2 marks each).

✗ Forgetting that third-party cookies track users across multiple websites — first-party cookies only affect the site that set them. Third-party (advertising) cookies follow users from site to site. This distinction is important for privacy questions.

✗ Saying 'targeted marketing is always bad' — the spec requires you to understand BOTH that targeted marketing is commercially valuable (businesses sell more; users see relevant ads) AND that it raises privacy and manipulation concerns.

✗ Not mentioning GDPR/data protection when discussing cookie consent — cookie banners exist because of legal obligations under data protection law. Always mention the legal requirement when discussing why sites ask for cookie consent.

13. Quick Reference — Key Terms & Revision

★ KEY TERMS — QUICK REFERENCE

E-commerce: The buying and selling of goods and services over the internet

Online Service: A service delivered via the internet — including shopping, banking, education, entertainment, news and booking systems

Shopping Basket / Cart: A temporary virtual container that stores selected items before checkout on an e-commerce site

Secure Checkout: A payment process using HTTPS encryption and authentication to protect card details during online transactions

Cookie: A small text file stored on a user's device by a website to remember their session, preferences or browsing activity

Session Cookie: A temporary cookie that is deleted when the browser is closed — used to maintain a shopping session

Persistent Cookie: A cookie with an expiry date that remains on the device between sessions — tracks repeat visits

Third-Party Cookie: A cookie set by an external advertiser (not the website being visited) — used for cross-site tracking

Transactional Data: Data generated by customer transactions — what was bought, when, for how much, by whom

Targeted Marketing: Delivering advertisements or promotions tailored to an individual based on their data profile

Personalisation: Customising the user experience based on data — recommendations, pricing, layout adapted per individual

Retargeting: Showing ads for products a user previously viewed on other websites they subsequently visit

Dynamic Pricing: Automatically adjusting prices based on demand, competition, time, or data about the customer

Cloud Computing: Delivering computing services (storage, applications, processing) over the internet from remote servers

Cloud Storage: Storing data on remote servers accessed via the internet (e.g. Google Drive, iCloud, OneDrive)

Hosted Application (SaaS): Software delivered as a service over the internet — no local installation required (e.g. Google Docs)

Locally Installed Software: Software downloaded and run directly on the user's own device or organisation's own servers

SaaS: Software as a Service — complete software delivered over the internet (e.g. Microsoft 365, Salesforce)

PaaS: Platform as a Service — cloud platform for developers to build and deploy applications

IaaS: Infrastructure as a Service — virtual hardware (servers, networking) rented on demand from cloud provider

Subscription Model: Paying a recurring fee (monthly/annual) for ongoing access to a service

On-Demand: Content or services available whenever the user wants, rather than at a fixed scheduled time

Streaming: Delivering audio or video content continuously in real time without requiring a full download first

⚡ Cloud vs Local — Quick Revision Summary

Cloud Storage: Stored on remote servers │ Accessed via internet │ Accessible anywhere │ Automatic backup │ Provider manages security │ Limited offline access

Local Storage: Stored on device/premises │ No internet needed │ One device typically │ User manages backup │ User controls security │ Fast local access

Hosted App (SaaS): Browser-based │ Auto-updated │ Internet required │ Subscription │ Easy collaboration │ Low hardware needs

Local App: Installed on device │ Manual updates │ Works offline │ One-off or subscription │ No collaboration by default │ High hardware needs

Cookies: Session (deleted when browser closes) │ Persistent (saved across visits) │ First-party (this site only) │ Third-party (cross-site tracking)

Transactional data: Purchases │ Browsing │ Searches │ Basket │ Device │ Location │ Account │ Social login

Marketing techniques: Recommendations │ Retargeting │ Email personalisation │ Dynamic pricing │ Loyalty programmes │ Social proof

⚡ Online Services — Eight Types Quick Summary

Shopping: Basket + checkout + secure payment + product catalogue — Amazon, ASOS, eBay

Booking: Real-time availability + instant confirmation + e-ticket — Expedia, Trainline, Ryanair

Banking: 24/7 access + instant transfers + MFA + transaction history — Barclays, Monzo

Education: VLE + on-demand courses + remote access + certification — Coursera, Google Classroom

Gaming: Multiplayer + digital downloads + subscriptions + in-game purchases — Steam, Xbox Game Pass

News: Live updates + multimedia + personalised feeds + archives — BBC, Guardian, Reuters

Auctions: Bidding system + buyer protection + seller ratings — eBay, Catawiki

Entertainment: On-demand + streaming + offline downloads + subscription — Netflix, Spotify, Disney+
