# The Three Answers: a Trimurti journal (treatment)

**Format:** 1080×1920 (9:16), 30 fps, 120 s · English voice-over, warm female narrator (Kokoro `af_bella`), burned-in subtitles + `.srt` · −14 LUFS
**Style:** Watercolor Brush (paper, self-painting translucent strokes with flying white, hand notes, no vector lines).
**An all-new film**, separate from the 60 s *Trimurti*. That one is a single vertical scroll of the cosmos. This one is a **naturalist's journal**: a handwritten question, then three locked pages, one per god, turned like pages, and the question page again at the end, now answered.

**Approach to the subject.** As before, symbols, not portraits: Brahma's lotus, the four Vedas, the hamsa; Vishnu's serpent seen from above, conch, discus, tilak and the ten avatars as small painted emblems; Shiva's crescent moon, matted hair catching the Ganga, the Nataraja ring of fire, drum and flame. The numbers are the traditional Puranic figures (a day of Brahma = 4.32 billion years; yugas of 1,728,000 / 1,296,000 / 864,000 / 432,000 years). The ninth avatar varies by tradition (Buddha or Balarama), and the film says so.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Single vertical cosmos** (ocean → lotus → mountain). Already the 60 s film. Rejected: an all-new film must not reuse it.
2. **A year-long journal**: one page per season, the gods mapped to seasons. Poetic, but it imposes a seasonal reading the tradition doesn't make.
3. **Three questions, three pages (chosen).** The opening page asks *Who makes the world? Who keeps it? Who ends it?* Each god gets a specimen page with notes and one data beat. The pages are filed into a stack, and the question page returns with the answers written beside it. It is a list that turns: the third page burns, and its ashes clear the paper for the answers.

## 2. Logline and arc

*A journal asks three questions about the world, and paints three answers: one who makes it, one who keeps it, one who ends it, so it can begin again.*
- **Setup:** the questions written by hand; Brahma's page: lotus, four Vedas, the hamsa, the world in a burst, the length of his day.
- **Middle:** Vishnu's page: the serpent mandala on the ocean, then ten emblems painted on the beat, one per age.
- **Turn:** Shiva's page: the Ganga caught in his hair, the ring of fire, the four ages shrinking and darkening; the page burns to ash.
- **Ending:** the three pages are filed; the question page slides back on top and each question receives its answer. Title.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Botanical and naturalist plates; Mughal and Rajput album pages** (grammar only). *Learn:* one specimen per page, numbered emblems, borders of notes, quiet margins, colour as classification. *Don't take:* any figure, border design, composition or specific painting.
- **Museum object labels / field notebooks.** *Learn:* the mono label + handwritten name pairing; numbers stated once, clearly.
