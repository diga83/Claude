# Finding Your Original Contribution — Strategy Memo

**Re:** *Mary Magdalene and the Memory of Women's Authority in Early Christianity*
**Date:** 4 June 2026

## The honest problem

The draft, as it stands, has no original contribution. Its three-part structure — Erasure → Heterodox Contestation → Feminist Reclamation — is a closed loop that roughly forty years of scholarship has already walked. A Scopus Q1 venue will not accept a paper for *summarising* this field, however well. Originality here cannot come from the *content* of those three boxes (all saturated); it has to come from a *frame or method that cuts across them*.

## What is already saturated (do not pitch into these)

The single most important strategic fact: the **_Oxford Handbook of Mary Magdalene_, ed. Diane Apostolos-Cappadona (OUP, 2024, with articles still appearing through 2025)** is now the field's saturation baseline. It has already claimed several angles that might have looked open:

- **The "seven demons" / mental-health / disability reading** — taken (Kate Mroz's Handbook chapter).
- **Global South / non-Western reception** — taken (Córdova Quero & Santos Meza on Ibero-America; African and diaspora reception).
- **Queer reception and visual reception** — taken (Handbook chapters; Apostolos-Cappadona's *Mary Magdalene: A Visual History*, 2023).
- **The core arc** (Gregory's 591 conflation → penitent-prostitute myth → feminist correction) — completely saturated. This is the current draft.
- **"In memory of her" / hermeneutic of remembrance** — owned by Schüssler Fiorenza and Mary Ann Beavis, but, crucially, only in the *metaphorical* register (see below).

Questions that are settled and therefore dead on arrival as theses: was Mary a prostitute (no); was she "apostle to the apostles" (yes); is the *Gospel of Mary* about women's authority (yes); was she erased and why (yes, in outline).

## The one genuinely open lane

Across every search, the recurring finding is this: **the field uses "memory" as a mood, not as a model.** Memory-studies theory has thoroughly colonised Historical-Jesus and Gospels research (Kelber; Sandra Hübenthal; the whole "Jesus and Memory" debate), and feminist scholarship invokes "memory" evocatively — but the two streams have *never been joined on the Magdalene with the actual theoretical apparatus*. That is the gap. Replacing the field's metaphorical "memory" with the real machinery of memory studies (Halbwachs's *cadres sociaux*; Jan Assmann's communicative vs. cultural memory and the "floating gap"; Aleida Assmann's storage vs. functional memory) is, as far as this survey can establish, the single open move on this otherwise crowded figure.

## Three candidate theses you could own

### Thesis A — "The Floating Gap" *(safest genuine win)*
**Claim:** *The Magdalene tradition is a textbook case of Jan Assmann's "floating gap" — the transition from living communicative memory to formalised cultural memory — and reading her this way explains why her authority was both preserved and neutralised in the same act of canonisation.*
- **Why it's novel:** it converts "erasure" (a moral claim) into "functional-memory selection" (a mechanism). That is a real analytic upgrade, not a relabelling.
- **What you'd add:** J. Assmann, *Cultural Memory and Early Civilization* and *Religion and Cultural Memory*; A. Assmann on *Speicher-/Funktionsgedächtnis*; Halbwachs; position yourself against the Hübenthal/Keith NT-memory volume; your existing feminist corpus becomes the data.
- **Novelty risk:** low–moderate.

### Thesis B — "One Mechanism, Many Women" *(most ambitious)*
**Claim:** *Magdalene, Junia, and Thecla were not erased by three coincidental accidents but by one recurring cultural-memory mechanism operating in three modes — scribal alteration, narrative conflation, and canonical domestication — and modelling that mechanism explains both the erasure and the predictable shape of the modern reclamation.*
- **Why it's novel:** the field studies each woman in a silo (Epp on Junia; the *Acts of Paul and Thecla* corpus; Phoebe scholarship). Nobody has built the comparative typology. Comparison is a recognised engine of originality precisely because this one is unbuilt.
- **What you'd add:** the single-figure literatures + Assmann/Halbwachs for the mechanism; the Schrader–Fellows debate as your Magdalene "scribal" case.
- **Novelty risk:** low for the *systematic* version.

### Thesis C — "The Tower and the Academy" *(most timely; shortest shelf life)*
**Claim:** *The reception of Elizabeth Schrader Polczer's "Mary the Tower" thesis — devotionally embraced by reclamation communities while contested by textual critics — is itself a live, observable cultural-memory event, in which a contested philological claim is canonised by a community's functional memory faster than the academy can adjudicate it.*
- **Why it's novel:** the current fight (Schrader, *HTR* 2017; Richard Fellows and Schrader's reply, *TC* 30, 2025; the "Rescuing Martha" critiques, 2025) is entirely text-critical. Nobody has stepped back to analyse the *reception of the thesis itself* as a memory event. You do not have to adjudicate whether Schrader is right — that neutrality is the strength.
- **Novelty risk:** moderate and *rising* — the topic is hot and moving.
