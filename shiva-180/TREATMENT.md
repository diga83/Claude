# The Paper Beneath: Shiva (treatment)

**Format:** 1080×1920 (9:16, like the rest of the series), 30 fps, 180 s · English voice-over, warm female narrator (Kokoro `af_bella`, speed 0.88), burned-in subtitles + `.srt` · −14 LUFS
**Style:** Watercolor Brush. **Approach:** symbols and places, not portraits. Content follows widely shared tradition: the churning of the ocean and Halahala (Neelakantha); Kailash, Adiyogi, Nataraja; the five Pancha Bhuta Sthalam temples (earth: Ekambareswarar, Kanchipuram; water: Jambukeswarar, Thiruvanaikaval; fire: Arunachaleswarar, Tiruvannamalai, with the Karthigai Deepam; air: Srikalahasteeswara, Srikalahasti; space: Thillai Nataraja, Chidambaram, and the Chidambara Rahasyam); the Panchakshara *Na-ma-shi-va-ya*, one syllable per element as usually given in Shaiva tradition.

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **Symbols plate** (moon, Ganga, third eye, trident, drum, snake, ash). Clear, but it repeats the Trimurti films' Shiva imagery.
2. **A story told in full** (the churning). Strong hook, but one myth can't carry three minutes.
3. **Poison, mountain, five elements, empty page (chosen).** The hook is the poison he holds in his throat. Then who he is (Kailash). Then a journey through the five elements, one temple each, each page washed away by the next element. The space temple reveals an empty space, and the five syllables of his mantra gather the elements back up. The ending is the medium itself: everything washes back to the paper, which "was always there", with one blue dot left from the opening.

## 2. Logline and arc
*He drinks the poison that would end the world; five temples find him in earth, water, fire, air and space; and the last thing they show is nothing at all.*
Setup: the churning, the poison, the blue throat. Development: Kailash; the five elements. Turn: Chidambaram's curtain opens on an empty space. Ending: the mantra's five syllables, then the page washes back to paper, with one blue dot left.

## 3. Benchmark
- **South Indian temple murals and Tanjore-style colour** (grammar only): warm ochres, vermilion, deep blues; tiered gopuram silhouettes. *Don't take:* any deity figure, mural composition or real temple drawing.
- **Botanical plates / museum labels:** element name, Sanskrit, place, one painted subject.

## 5. Cue map
72 BPM (beat 0.833 s, bar 3.333 s), D minor over a tanpura-like D–A drone (cellos + a plucked harp figure). Flute melody; frame drum from the fire page through Nataraja; Nepalese bells at Chidambaram; a soft note after each syllable (never on it); a tubular bell under the title. Two silences: before the curtain parts, and before the last line.
