# Trimurti — treatment

**Format:** 1080×1920 (9:16), 30 fps, ~61 s · English voice-over, warm female narrator (Kokoro `af_bella`, speed 0.88), burned-in subtitles + `.srt` · −14 LUFS
**Style:** Watercolor Brush (fixed invariants from `STYLE.md`: warm cotton paper, self-painting translucent strokes with dry-brush flying white, hand-written notes, no vector lines).

**Approach to the subject.** The Trimurti are painted as **their symbols, not as portraits**: Brahma's lotus, Vishnu's serpent-coil, conch and discus on the cosmic ocean, Shiva's mountain, crescent moon, trident and ring of fire. That keeps the devotional figures dignified (no code-drawn faces) and plays to the style's specimen-and-note grammar. The narration holds to the widely shared reading: Brahma creates, Vishnu preserves, Shiva destroys and transforms, and the cycle turns again.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Specimen triptych.** A locked journal page, three plates side by side: lotus, discus, trident, each with its note. Clear, but static, and a list doesn't *turn*. It never shows the cycle.
2. **Before/after on one view.** One landscape painted, kept, then burned and regrown. Strong as a cycle, but it hides the three as characters in weather.
3. **One tall painted world, read as a cycle (chosen).** The 9:16 frame becomes a vertical handscroll: ocean at the bottom (Vishnu), the lotus and the living world in the middle (Brahma), Kailash and the moon at the top (Shiva). The camera **rises with creation, sinks to the preserver, climbs to the destroyer**, then the whole sheet washes back to paper, and the first drop falls again. The vertical frame *is* the cosmology, and the ending bookends the opening.

Why 3: it gives one subject (the world) one turn (dissolution) and an echoing ending, and uses the tall frame as a tilt rather than fighting it.

## 2. Logline and arc

*A single drop of colour becomes a world. One god paints it, one keeps it, one dances it away, so that the drop can fall again.*

- **Setup:** drop on blank paper → the cosmic ocean → a lotus rises; Brahma creates; the world bursts into colour.
- **Turn:** Vishnu holds it from below; then, in silence, the climb to Shiva. His drum, his ring of fire; the world mist-washes back to paper (the native move, at the peak).
- **Ending:** on the bare sheet the three emblems paint in as one circle: three faces of one cosmos. A last drop falls; a new lotus bud starts, and the brush lifts mid-stroke.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Indian miniature & Kangra-school pages; Pichhwai temple cloths** (grammar only): stacked vertical registers, water drawn as rhythmic bands, lotus as a repeated sign.
  *Learn:* vertical registers as a map of the cosmos; pattern-as-water; flat, devotional calm. *Don't take:* any figure, composition, border or specific painting.
- **Field-journal watercolour plates:** specimen + hand note + leader line. *Learn:* one hero object per view, generous margins.

## 8. Type

- Subtitles: Cormorant Garamond italic, ~52 px at 1080 wide (scaled for vertical), centred in the lower margin on paper, ink at 78 % over a paper halo, max 2 lines.
- Notes: Caveat, ink brown, written left to right with a pen leader line to the emblem.
- Measured labels: IBM Plex Mono small caps with wide tracking (`CREATION · PRESERVATION · DISSOLUTION`).
- Title: TRIMURTI in Cormorant Garamond, letter-spaced, with *the holy triad* in Caveat beneath.
- Accent: **vermilion `#c8442a`**, the spark that recurs: the lotus heart, the discus rim, Shiva's fire, the final drop's centre.

Palette: paper `#f3eadb`; ocean indigo `#2f4c6e` / `#6f8faa`; lotus pink `#e2a0a8` / `#b8606f`; sage `#8ea860`, ochre `#c99a5b`; Kailash blue-grey `#9aa7b4`; ink `#3a2c22`.
