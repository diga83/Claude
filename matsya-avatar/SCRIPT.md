# Matsya Avatar — The Fish Who Saved the World

**Format:** 9:16 vertical (1080×1920, 30 fps) · **Length:** 89.5 s · **Narration:** soft female voice (Kokoro, blend of *af_heart* + *af_nicole*) · **Style:** "Luminous Myth" — flat 2D shapes in jewel-tone gradients (saffron, magenta, royal purple, teal, gold), gold-line mandalas, soft glows, slow camera pushes and cross-dissolves.

**Score & sound:** original procedural score — tanpura-style drone in D, a slow flute melody in raga Bhupali, harp plucks; a dark drone for the demon and a frame-drum pulse for the storm. Effects: conch, temple bell, chimes, rain, thunder, waves.

## Narration (full text)

Long ago, a gentle king named Satyavrata knelt by a river to offer water in prayer. A tiny fish shimmered in his cupped hands, and whispered, please, protect me. So the king carried it home in a water pot. But by morning, the fish had outgrown it. He moved it to a pond, then a lake, then a mighty river, until even the ocean could barely hold it. The king bowed and asked, who are you, really? The golden fish replied, I am Vishnu, protector of the worlds. In seven days, a great flood will swallow the earth. Gather the seven sages, the seeds of every plant, and every creature, and wait for me. Meanwhile, a demon named Hayagriva stole the sacred Vedas, and hid them deep beneath the sea. On the seventh day, the skies broke open, and the oceans rose above the mountains. Just then, a shining boat appeared, and so did Matsya, now vast as a mountain, with one golden horn. Using the serpent Vasuki as a rope, the king tied the boat to that horn. All through the stormy night, Matsya pulled them across the waves. Then he dove into the deep, defeated the demon, and carried the Vedas back into the light. When the waters settled, a new dawn rose, and life began again. This is Matsya, the first avatar of Vishnu, a reminder that one small act of kindness can save the world.
