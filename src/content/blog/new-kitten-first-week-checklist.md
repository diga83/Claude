The first week with a kitten sets the tone for the next fifteen-plus years. Here's the checklist we wish every new owner had — what to prepare, what to expect, and the classic mistakes to skip.

## Before the kitten arrives

**Buy:**

- Litter box with **low sides** and non-clumping or pellet litter for kittens under 8 weeks ([why clumping litter can be risky](/kittens/kitten-litter-training/))
- Kitten food — wet food labeled "for growth" or "all life stages" ([why kitten food matters](/kittens/kitten-vs-adult-food/))
- Shallow food and water dishes (whisker-friendly)
- A carrier — leave it out with a blanket inside from day one, not just for vet trips
- Scratching post, a few toys (wand toy, kickers), and a cozy bed they will ignore in favor of a cardboard box

**Kitten-proof one room.** Not the whole house — one quiet "base camp" room. Hide cords, hair ties, strings, and plants. Get on the floor and look at the room from kitten height; you'll find hazards you missed. (Full list: [how to kitten-proof your home](/kittens/kitten-proofing-home/).)

## Day one: less is more

The biggest day-one mistake is the welcome party. Your kitten just lost its mother, littermates, and entire known world. Put it in the base-camp room with food, water, litter, and a hiding spot — then **let it come to you**. Sit on the floor, talk softly, play with a wand toy if it's curious. Hiding under the bed for the first day or two is completely normal.

Expect crying the first few nights. A warm snuggle bed, a ticking clock or heartbeat toy, and a t-shirt that smells like you all help. ([Why kittens cry at night](/kittens/why-does-my-kitten-cry/).)

## The first week

1. **Book the vet visit** within the first few days: health check, parasite treatment, vaccine schedule, and a chat about [spay/neuter timing](/kittens/when-to-spay-neuter-kitten/) — female kittens can get pregnant at **four months**, so this is not a "later" conversation.
2. **Feed small and often** — 3–4 meals a day for most kittens. ([Feeding schedule by age](/kittens/how-often-feed-kitten/).)
3. **Handle everything gently** — paws, ears, mouth — paired with treats. Sixty seconds a day now buys you a lifetime of easy nail trims and vet visits. You're inside the [socialization window](/kittens/kitten-socialization-window/); use it.
4. **Teach bite rules from day one**: hands are never toys. When teeth touch skin, go still and boring, then redirect to a toy. ([Full method](/kittens/kitten-biting-how-to-stop/).)
5. **Expand territory gradually** — once the kitten owns its room confidently, open the door and let it explore outward at its own pace.

## The mistakes everyone makes

- **Free-feeding from a giant bowl** — you lose the single best health signal (appetite) and start the obesity clock early.
- **Punishing accidents** — litter misses are information (box too far? sides too tall?), never defiance.
- **Skipping pet insurance research** — if you're going to insure, the cheapest premiums of the cat's life are right now, before anything becomes "pre-existing." ([Is insurance worth it?](/health/is-pet-insurance-worth-it/))
- **Buying one of everything** — buy cheap and varied first; let the kitten vote, then invest in what it actually uses.

A week of patience now produces a confident, handleable, well-adjusted cat. And if you're still deciding *whether* to get one kitten or two — [read this first](/kittens/single-kitten-syndrome/). Two is often genuinely easier.
