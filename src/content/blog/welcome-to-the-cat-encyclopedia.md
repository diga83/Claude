Every cat owner has done it: it's 2am, your cat is yowling at a wall, and you're typing *"why is my cat staring at nothing"* into a search engine — then wading through ten pages of ads to find half an answer.

We built the Cat Encyclopedia to fix that. **333 answers across 14 topics**, written to be clear, honest about uncertainty, and aligned with mainstream veterinary guidance. No twenty-paragraph preamble before the answer. No "as an avid cat lover" filler.

## How to use it

- **Search first.** Press <kbd>⌘K</kbd> (or tap the search bar) and type your question the way you'd actually ask it — *"can cats eat cheese"*, *"why does my cat bite me"*. The search understands natural phrasing.
- **Browse by topic** when you want depth: every topic page covers its subject end to end, from [behavior](/behavior/) to [toxic plants](/toxic/) to [senior care](/senior/).
- **Share answers.** Every answer has its own page and a copy-link button — perfect for the group chat debate about whether milk is good for cats. (It isn't. [Here's why](/can-cats-eat/can-cats-drink-milk/).)

## What's coming on this blog

The encyclopedia answers questions; the blog goes deeper. Expect:

- Seasonal guides (holiday hazards, summer heat, moving house with a cat)
- Deep dives on the science behind the answers
- New-owner starter guides
- Honest looks at contested topics — raw feeding, indoor vs outdoor, breed welfare

## One important note

Nothing here is veterinary advice. We'll say this often, because it matters: a website can summarize what's typically true, but only a vet can examine *your* cat. When in doubt, call the professional.
